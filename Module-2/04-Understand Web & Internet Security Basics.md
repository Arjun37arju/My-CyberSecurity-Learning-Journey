## a. Surface Web

### Content

The **Surface Web** is the part of the Internet that is **publicly accessible and indexed by search engines** such as Google and Bing.

Examples include:

* Public websites
* News websites
* Blogs
* Public documentation
* Public social media pages

```text id="srf23k"
Internet
   ↓
Surface Web
   ↓
Search Engines
   ↓
Public Websites
```

Example:

> Searching Google for a public cybersecurity article and opening the website is an example of using the Surface Web.

**Easy idea:**

> **Surface Web = Public websites that search engines can index.**

## b. Deep Web

### Content

The **Deep Web** refers to Internet content that **is not indexed by normal search engines**.

This does **not** mean it is illegal or dangerous.

Examples include:

* Online banking accounts
* Private email inboxes
* Private databases
* Company internal systems
* Content behind login pages

```text id="dp92mx"
Internet
   ↓
Deep Web
   ↓
Login / Private Access
   ↓
Private Information
```

Example:

> Your private email inbox is part of the Deep Web because search engines cannot normally index the contents of your account.

**Easy idea:**

> **Deep Web = Internet content not normally indexed by search engines.**

## c. Dark Web

### Content

The **Dark Web** is a portion of the Internet that is **intentionally hidden and requires special software or configurations to access**.

It is commonly associated with privacy-focused networks such as **Tor**.

The Dark Web can be used for legitimate purposes, such as:

* Privacy
* Anonymous communication
* Circumventing censorship
* Protecting journalists and sources

However, it can also be used for illegal activities.

```text id="dkw7p4"
Internet
   ↓
Dark Web
   ↓
Special Network / Software
   ↓
Hidden Services
```

**Easy idea:**

> **Dark Web = Intentionally hidden Internet services requiring special access.**

## d. Anonymous Browsing Concepts

### Content

**Anonymous browsing** refers to techniques intended to **reduce the amount of identifying information exposed while browsing the Internet**.

However, complete anonymity is difficult to guarantee.

Different technologies can provide different levels of privacy.

Examples include:

* Privacy-focused browsers
* VPNs
* Tor
* Browser privacy settings

```text id="an7qk6"
User
  ↓
Privacy Technology
  ↓
Internet
  ↓
Website
```

Each technology works differently and protects different types of information.

For example, a VPN can help hide your IP address from websites and encrypt traffic between your device and the VPN server, but it does **not make you completely anonymous**.

**Easy idea:**

> **Anonymous Browsing = Reducing identifying information while browsing, not guaranteed complete anonymity.**

## e. Online Privacy Awareness

### Content

**Online privacy awareness** means understanding **what personal information you share online, who can access it, and how it can be used**.

Information that may reveal details about you includes:

* Name
* Email address
* Phone number
* Location information
* Photos
* Browsing activity
* Account information

```text id="op5r8w"
Your Information
       ↓
Online Services
       ↓
Collection / Storage
       ↓
Possible Privacy Risks
```

Good privacy practices include:

* Use strong and unique passwords
* Enable multi-factor authentication
* Review privacy settings
* Avoid oversharing personal information
* Be careful with suspicious links
* Check what permissions applications request
* Keep software updated

**Easy idea:**

> **Online Privacy = Understand and control what personal information you share online.**
