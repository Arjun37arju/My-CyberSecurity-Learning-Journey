# Understand OSINT Fundamentals

**OSINT (Open-Source Intelligence)** is the process of **collecting, analyzing, and organizing information from publicly available sources** to answer a specific question or understand a situation.

OSINT is widely used in cybersecurity, journalism, investigations, threat intelligence, and research.

## a. Open-Source Intelligence

### Content

**Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT)** is intelligence produced from information that is **legally and publicly accessible**.

Sources can include:

* Websites
* Search engines
* Public reports
* News articles
* Public databases
* Security advisories
* Public social media posts
* Government publications

```text id="os1k7m"
Public Sources
      ↓
Collect Information
      ↓
Analyze Information
      ↓
Connect Findings
      ↓
OSINT
```

OSINT is not simply collecting information. The information must be **analyzed and connected to produce useful findings**.

**Easy idea:**

> **OSINT = Collect + Analyze public information to produce useful intelligence.**

## b. Public Information Gathering

### Content

**Public information gathering** is the process of collecting information that is openly available to the public.

In cybersecurity, this can help researchers understand an organization's **publicly visible attack surface and exposure**.

Examples of public information include:

* Organization websites
* Public domains
* Public subdomains
* Technology information
* Public documents
* Security advisories
* Public contact information

```text id="p4v7x2"
Public Sources
      ↓
Information Gathering
      ↓
Organize Findings
      ↓
Analyze
```

**Easy idea:**

> **Public Information Gathering = Collecting information that is openly available.**

## c. Search Techniques

### Content

**Search techniques** are methods used to find relevant information efficiently.

Instead of using only simple searches, researchers can use more precise search queries.

For example:

```text id="s8x2n5"
General Search
      ↓
More Specific Keywords
      ↓
Search Operators
      ↓
Relevant Results
      ↓
Verify Information
```

Useful search operators include:

### `site:`

Search within a specific website or domain.

```text
site:example.com cybersecurity
```

### `"quotes"`

Search for an exact phrase.

```text
"cybersecurity incident"
```

### `-`

Exclude a term.

```text
cybersecurity -jobs
```

### `filetype:`

Search for a particular file type.

```text
filetype:pdf cybersecurity report
```

These techniques help researchers reduce irrelevant search results.

**Easy idea:**

> **Search Techniques = Use precise queries to find relevant information faster.**

## d. Threat Research

### Content

**Threat research** is the process of collecting and analyzing information about cybersecurity threats.

A threat can include:

* Malware
* Phishing campaigns
* Vulnerabilities
* Threat actors
* Attack techniques
* Cyber incidents

```text id="t7m3q9"
Threat
  ↓
Collect Information
  ↓
Research Sources
  ↓
Analyze
  ↓
Identify Impact
  ↓
Security Findings
```

Threat researchers use sources such as:

* Security vendor reports
* CVE databases
* Security advisories
* Government cybersecurity publications
* Incident reports
* Research papers

**Easy idea:**

> **Threat Research = Understand a threat using reliable information and evidence.**

## e. Investigation Workflows

### Content

An **investigation workflow** is a structured process used to collect, verify, analyze, and document information.

A basic OSINT investigation can follow:

```text id="w6p9r3"
Define Question
      ↓
Collect Information
      ↓
Evaluate Sources
      ↓
Verify Findings
      ↓
Analyze Information
      ↓
Document Results
```

### 1. Define the Question

Clearly identify what you are trying to find out.

### 2. Collect Information

Gather relevant information from public sources.

### 3. Evaluate Sources

Check whether the sources are reliable and relevant.

### 4. Verify Findings

Cross-check important information using multiple sources.

### 5. Analyze

Connect the information and identify useful findings.

### 6. Document

Record the findings, evidence, sources, and conclusions.

**Easy idea:**

> **Investigation Workflow = Question → Collect → Verify → Analyze → Document.**
